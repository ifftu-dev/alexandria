# Skill Graph visual research

**Researched:** 2026-10-27  
**Scope:** Visual presentation and navigation over Alexandria's existing taxonomy, prerequisites, and credential-derived state. No application changes. The design brief below was confirmed by the user after the design interview; implementation has not started.  
**Method:** Primary sources only: product documentation, first-party screenshots, W3C guidance, a paper by a taxonomy author, and animation research by its authors. “Documented” means stated by the source; “observed” means visible in a screenshot inspected during this research; “proposal” means a design inference requiring validation. No live Alexandria UI was tested.

## Confirmed design brief

The user confirmed this shared understanding after the grilling interview. These decisions supersede the earlier exploratory mockups wherever they differ.

- **Structure and layout:** preserve every skill, taxonomy membership, and prerequisite relationship. Reposition nodes into stable, named subject regions; keep cross-field connections discoverable and positions steady through ordinary exploration.
- **Always-visible meaning:** subject color identifies the field. Every earned node displays the learner's **current earned Bloom number and name**, plus a separate earned checkmark. This is credential-evidenced Bloom, not the skill's intrinsic category and not the derived 1–5 score band. Use mostly uniform node sizes.
- **Unearned skills:** every unearned skill is **Ready**, never Locked. Do not invent an earned Bloom number for it. Its learning-outcome Bloom category belongs in the inspector. Skills whose prerequisites are already earned may receive stronger emphasis, but other skills remain visible, explorable, and never presented as inaccessible.
- **Two equal purposes:** show what the learner has earned across fields and what else they can learn. Include opportunities to deepen already-earned skills without inventing a universal six-step advancement ladder.
- **Navigation:** clicking selects a node and opens an inspector; a separate Open skill action navigates away. Preserve the camera and selection on return. Include field focus, search, prerequisite/dependent navigation, and overview/reset controls.
- **Responsive inspection:** desktop side panel; mobile bottom sheet with a collapsed state that leaves room to explore. Share information and actions across both.
- **Motion:** remain still once settled. Use brief navigation transitions and localized feedback for newly earned skills or earned-level improvements. No continuous floating or pulsing; honor reduced motion.
- **First implementation scope:** personal graph page, expanded modal, and matching sidebar treatment. Public instructor graphs are deferred. Existing guided-path restrictions are a separate behavior follow-up, not part of this graph redesign.

The closest research reference is Open Knowledge Maps' overview/detail pattern; Obsidian and Kumu supply local-neighborhood navigation; Cosmograph supplies inspectable selections and legends. These are complementary interaction ideas, not a proposal to adopt their graph models or replace Alexandria's renderer. Source evidence follows.

## Fit to Alexandria's current data and presentation

These are source observations, not a full audit:

- [`SkillInfo`](../src/types/index.ts) already includes `subject_id`, `subject_name`, `subject_field_id`, `subject_field_name`, and `bloom_level`. This supports a field/subject display hierarchy without new taxonomy entities. Nullable membership needs a visible “Unassigned” group; do not silently omit those skills.
- [`SidebarSkillGraph.vue`](../src/components/layout/SidebarSkillGraph.vue) derives earned/available/locked state and maps skills to graph nodes. That mapping currently passes Bloom level and prerequisite IDs, but omits subject and field metadata. **Proposal:** carry existing membership through a presentation adapter; no database migration is implied.
- [`SkillGraphModal.vue`](../src/components/layout/SkillGraphModal.vue) constructs links from prerequisite to dependent, uses a force layout, fits the graph when the engine stops, and navigates to the skill page on node click. **Proposal:** make first activation select/inspect and provide a distinct “Open skill” action, so exploration does not immediately leave the map.
- [`useSkillGraphHover.ts`](../src/composables/useSkillGraphHover.ts) already fades unrelated nodes, reveals labels by zoom/hover, and includes label-collision handling. It paints status colors and draws plain link lines. [`bloom.ts`](../src/utils/bloom.ts) provides six ordered categories, colors, and radius helpers. **Proposal:** retain useful hover behavior while making selection persistent and explaining each encoding.
- [`useSkillGraphState.ts`](../src/composables/useSkillGraphState.ts) distinguishes credential claim Bloom ranks from derived aggregation score bands in comments/helpers. The source audit below confirms that the sidebar nevertheless indexes Bloom names using the derived score band; preserve the distinction in the redesign. [`forceGraph.ts`](../src/utils/forceGraph.ts) is the renderer integration boundary, not a reason to replace the rendering library.

## Concrete visual and interaction proposals

### 1. Make fields visible without pretending the taxonomy is a prerequisite graph

Use labeled field regions as stable landmarks. Within each region, show an earned count such as “8 of 24 skills earned” and a small set of named earned skills; disclose truncation with “+5 more.” Counts must use distinct existing skill IDs and existing earned-state semantics. If the loaded dataset is partial, label counts “loaded skills” rather than implying complete coverage.

Expand a field into subjects and then individual skill nodes. Use containment, headings, or a background boundary for membership. Draw prerequisite edges only from actual edge records. A visual field hub, if needed, must look like a navigation control and must never appear to be an earnable skill. This adapts [VIPER's overview/detail pattern](https://openknowledgemaps.org/preview/viper/about); the exact design is a proposal.

For the compact sidebar, favor a quiet field summary with earned markers and a clear expand action. Reserve edge-heavy exploration for the modal. Preserve field placement between visits instead of reshuffling landmarks whenever credentials change. This stability is a design hypothesis to validate, not a measured result from the references.

### 2. Give each visual channel one meaning

**Confirmed encoding:** a stable categorical hue identifies each subject field, repeated in its region and field selector. An earned skill has a filled core and separate checkmark; every unearned skill is labeled Ready and uses an open marker. Selection uses a separate focus outline. Prerequisite satisfaction can influence emphasis but must not introduce a Locked label or imply access restrictions.

Show the learner's **current earned Bloom category** continuously on each earned node: a compact 1–6 category marker plus its level name, backed by a persistent six-level legend. Keep these markers at a readable screen size when zooming. These numbers label categories, not a percentage or score. Keep the skill's intrinsic category separately labeled in the inspector; do not substitute it when no earned level exists. Subject color remains stable without requiring a color-mode switch to see Bloom.

Categorical field colors follow ColorBrewer's distinction between qualitative palettes for categories and sequential palettes for ordered values. [ColorBrewer guidance](https://colorbrewer2.org/learnmore/schemes.html). Legend controls may highlight matching earned Bloom categories, with a reset action and visible filter state, adapting [Cosmograph's categorical legend behavior](https://next.cosmograph.app/docs/app/how-to-use/).

Use text and shape as redundant cues because color alone is insufficient. [W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html). Do not finalize hex values by copying screenshots: validate colors, outlines, labels, and links in both Alexandria themes. Required non-text cues generally need 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors, subject to the criterion's exceptions. [W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).

### 3. Make navigation explicit and reversible

Provide breadcrumbs, search with field/subject context, a reset-view button, and a persistent skill inspector. From a selected skill, expose “Prerequisites” and “Dependents” as separate lists and map controls. Offer one-hop focus first and optional expansion, adapting [Kumu focus](https://docs.kumu.io/guides/focus) and [Obsidian local graph](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph).

Keep off-field prerequisite connections discoverable through labeled boundary markers or an “outside this field” list. Never make a filtered-out prerequisite look satisfied. Preserve camera position and selection when returning from detail. In the inspector, show full name, taxonomy path, current earned Bloom when present, separately labeled learning-outcome Bloom category, Earned/Ready state, prerequisite context, and an explicit route to the skill page. Use a desktop side panel and a mobile bottom sheet with a collapsed state. Offer relevant learning opportunities for both new skills and further development of earned skills.

Provide equivalent search/list selection for keyboard and assistive-technology users, visible focus, zoom buttons, and click/tap alternatives to custom drag-only navigation. Keyboard access and non-drag pointer operation are separate requirements. [W3C 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html), [2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html). Avoid essential hover-only information; hover/focus popups must satisfy applicable dismissible, hoverable, and persistent behavior. [W3C 1.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html).

### 4. Animate changes of context, then let the graph rest

**Proposed starting values, not standards:** a short 150–200 ms emphasis fade, roughly 300–450 ms field-to-detail camera transition, and a single 500–700 ms earned-state highlight. Keep the selected node visually anchored. Avoid continuous orbiting, breathing status glows, bouncing labels, and automatic camera travel after every small update. A one-time earned-state or earned-level-improvement highlight can acknowledge new evidence without replaying every time the view opens. Heer and Robertson's experiments support carefully designed transitions for statistical graphics, but are not direct evidence that these timings improve Alexandria's skill graph; validate the transfer. [Authors' research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/animated-transitions-in-statistical-data-graphics/).

Honor reduced motion in both CSS and canvas/JavaScript behavior: skip interpolated camera travel and decorative motion, apply state changes directly, and expose an application motion control. Interaction-triggered nonessential motion being disableable is WCAG 2.3.3 **AAA**, not an AA requirement. [W3C motion guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html). Automatically moving content lasting over five seconds alongside other content falls under the conditions of WCAG 2.2.2 **A**, with an essential-activity exception. [Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html).

Stop simulation/redraw work when settled or hidden where feasible; animation quality needs profiling on target devices. For small nodes, use a usable screen-space hit target or equivalent list control. WCAG 2.5.8 **AA** specifies a 24×24 CSS-pixel target minimum with spacing, equivalent-control, and other exceptions; it does not require every drawn node circle to be 24 pixels across. [Target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).

## Bloom and graph-semantic guardrails

**Primary educational source:** David R. Krathwohl, *A Revision of Bloom's Taxonomy: An Overview* (2002), especially p. 215. The author describes separate knowledge and cognitive-process dimensions and explicitly relaxes a strict hierarchy to allow category overlap. [Author's paper, institutional-hosted copy](https://centreforfacdev.ca/pdf/TLC%20Resources/A%20Revision%20of%20Bloom_s%20Taxonomy%20-%20An%20Overview.pdf), [publication record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1477405).

**Design implications:**

- Treat Bloom as ordered categories for presentation, not an equal-interval score, percentage complete, or universal ladder every learner must climb. A larger “Create” node must not imply a more proficient learner or a more valuable skill. Prefer uniform node size with an explicit category badge; if size remains, explain its limited meaning.
- Never average Bloom ranks into a field “mastery” score. Do not invent six-stage progress rings from a single categorical label. The paper's two dimensions do not authorize adding new domain structure in this visuals-only task.
- Keep intrinsic skill Bloom, credential-evidenced Bloom, derived proficiency, confidence, and earned status separately named. “No evidence” must not silently become “Remember,” zero proficiency, or a learner deficit. Reuse existing authoritative state; flag ambiguities for the parent audit.
- Spatial proximity in a force layout is not measured conceptual similarity, readiness, or prerequisite strength. Open Knowledge Maps explicitly documents its own similarity-based layout and warns that centrality is not importance and within-area item placement has no specific meaning. Those semantics cannot simply be transplanted into Alexandria. [OKMaps FAQ](https://openknowledgemaps.org/faq).
- Separate membership from prerequisite direction. If a view hides edges for legibility, expose the actual relationships in the inspector; do not fabricate routes from layout geometry. Likewise, InfraNodus community detection or structural gaps must not create or remove Alexandria prerequisite edges. [InfraNodus explanation](https://infranodus.com/docs/gephi-alternative-network-analysis).

## Verified implementation audit

Source reviewed in the current working tree on 2026-10-27; these are code observations, not live UI measurements.

1. **There are multiple graph surfaces.** The personal [`skills/Index.vue`](../src/pages/skills/Index.vue) graph and expanded modal share the status-colored canvas renderer. The sidebar has its own compact status renderer. The Bloom-colored layered SVG [`SkillGraph.vue`](../src/components/skills/SkillGraph.vue) is used by [`u/InstructorGraph.vue`](../src/pages/u/InstructorGraph.vue), not the personal graph tab. Align their legend vocabulary while respecting different viewing contexts.
2. **Field identity is available but discarded in the personal graph adapters.** `SkillInfo` has both subject and field metadata. The sidebar and personal graph mappings omit it. The public instructor adapter currently fills `subject_field_id` and `subject_field_name` with `null`; field coloring there needs an existing local-taxonomy lookup by skill ID with an explicit unassigned fallback. Do not invent a classification or require a wire-contract change for the first visual pass.
3. **Search and field navigation exist in Browse, not on the graph itself.** The personal page keeps those controls inside its Browse tab. Its graph inherits the filtered skills when initialized. Put current filter state, search, and reset directly beside the graph so a previous Browse selection cannot create an unexplained partial view. [Page source](../src/pages/skills/Index.vue).
4. **The two large personal graphs leave the map on click.** Both route to a skill detail page; the modal also closes. Keep selection in place, expose an explicit open-details action, and retain camera/selection when returning. The sidebar disables panning, zooming, and node dragging and offers a small expand button/double-click; make expanding its primary, clearly labeled action. [Sidebar](../src/components/layout/SidebarSkillGraph.vue), [modal](../src/components/layout/SkillGraphModal.vue), [personal page](../src/pages/skills/Index.vue).
5. **Layout polish differs across surfaces.** The modal installs label-box collision and label-aware link distances; the personal page only installs charge and a fixed link distance. Both create/destroy instances and fit on engine stop. Share a visual layout configuration and preserve coordinates keyed by skill ID, while keeping prerequisite data unchanged. [Modal](../src/components/layout/SkillGraphModal.vue), [page](../src/pages/skills/Index.vue), [collision implementation](../src/composables/useSkillGraphHover.ts).
6. **Bloom and proficiency are currently conflated in one adapter.** The backend explicitly defines `DerivedSkillState.level` as a **1–5 score band**, calculated from raw evidence scores, while the sidebar uses `BLOOM_ORDER[st.level]` to display proficiency and defaults missing derived state to Remember. This is not the same as a credential's **0–5 Bloom claim**. Use separately labeled fields in the inspector and an existing credential-claim helper for evidenced Bloom; do not reinterpret the aggregation model. The personal page does not currently supply the renderer's `proficiency` field, so its shared hover path falls back to “Unknown” even for earned nodes. [Aggregation contract](../src-tauri/src/aggregation/mod.rs), [score-band mapping](../src-tauri/src/aggregation/level.rs), [sidebar adapter](../src/components/layout/SidebarSkillGraph.vue), [Bloom-claim helpers](../src/composables/useSkillGraphState.ts), [page adapter](../src/pages/skills/Index.vue), [hover labels](../src/composables/useSkillGraphHover.ts).
7. **Motion and appearance need intentional handling.** The canvas renderer uses per-frame interpolation and hard-coded dark backplates; large views use `autoPauseRedraw(false)`. No reduced-motion branch appears in these graph components/renderer. Implement elapsed-time transitions, settle/stop idle redraws when appropriate, and resolve graph colors from theme tokens. Profile before claiming a battery or performance improvement. [Renderer](../src/composables/useSkillGraphHover.ts), [modal](../src/components/layout/SkillGraphModal.vue), [page](../src/pages/skills/Index.vue).
8. **A useful fixture already exists.** The checked-in [`public_taxonomy.json`](../bootstrap/public_taxonomy.json) contains 8 subject fields, 25 subjects, 103 skills, and 89 prerequisite records at inspection time. These are fixture counts, not the user's live graph size or achievement counts. Benchmark the actual loaded graph separately.

## Implementation order and visual acceptance checks

These are recommended stages for a later implementation, not changes made by this research.

1. **Meaning and subject recognition:** carry existing field metadata into graph nodes; use field colors, field labels, distinct earned markers, always-visible current earned Bloom markers and names, separately labeled scores, and earned counts by field. Label all unearned skills Ready; use emphasis rather than access language for prerequisite satisfaction. Keep unavailable membership visible as Unassigned.
2. **Navigation:** persistent selection and a desktop side inspector/mobile bottom sheet, graph-local search, current filter state, field focus, prerequisite/dependent lists, reset/Back, and camera restoration. Preserve cross-field prerequisite context. Make all core tasks possible through native keyboard-accessible controls.
3. **Readability across surfaces:** shared collision/label policy, uniform screen-space hit targets, readable names at useful zoom levels, theme-aware styling, and a compact sidebar summary. At overview scale prioritize field names and earned regions; reveal skill names progressively and retain full names in the inspector/list.
4. **Motion and performance:** anchored camera transitions, brief emphasis and achievement feedback, preserved positions through updates, reduced motion, and redraws that stop when idle. Keep the current renderer initially; replace it only if measured limitations remain.

Usability checks for a later app prototype: identify the fields containing earned skills; locate a named skill; explain its field, intrinsic Bloom level and evidenced Bloom without confusing them; inspect an unmet prerequisite outside the active field; return to the previous position. Repeat with keyboard, touch, reduced motion, and both themes. Include no-earned-skills, unassigned membership, long labels, partial data, dense cross-field edges, and the complete bootstrap fixture. These are proposed checks, not completed usability research.

## Guided-path follow-up boundary

The focused source audit found no graph-status gate in node navigation or general course enrollment. However, goal-assessment planning marks skills with unmet prerequisites unassessable, and the learning-path UI filters assessment/course actions. The user explicitly agreed to address these restrictions separately. Renaming graph states must not claim to have removed them.

## Exploratory concepts delivered during research

Two self-contained examples are stored outside the application repo: `../../work/skill-graph-research/skill-atlas.html` and `../../work/skill-graph-research/skill-atlas-earned.html`. Both use 13 actual taxonomy skill names and the 12 actual prerequisite links induced by that subset. Their 8 earned skills and demonstrated Bloom levels are **illustrative**, not read from a learner profile. The second example switches the always-visible level to **earned Bloom**, adds learning-opportunity emphasis, and demonstrates stable subject regions. Previewed at 728px and 360px; interaction checks passed for earned-level display, separation from intrinsic category, next-skill selection, prerequisite navigation, and overview reset.

These are exploratory mockups, not the final implementation brief. In particular, they predate the confirmed decision that **all unearned skills are Ready**; any Locked labels in the examples are superseded. They also do not implement the agreed desktop side panel/mobile bottom sheet. There is no backend, freeform pan/pinch navigation, full subject drill-down, camera-history stack, or full-taxonomy density/performance testing. Follow the confirmed brief above for implementation.

## Limitations

The shortlist is selected for relevance to this task, not a comparative usability study or vendor ranking. First-party product statements establish documented behavior, not independent evidence of effectiveness. Four products' static visuals were inspected; two interfaces were documentation-only. The Graph Commons navigation/customization deep links returned errors, so only directly observed demo semantics are claimed. No accessibility conformance, performance benchmark, live animation behavior, or production Alexandria appearance was verified. Renderer selection, backend changes, AI-derived relationships, and credential-policy changes are outside scope.
