# Skill graph UI research: crafted graph interfaces

Status: reference research

**Researched:** 2026-11-20
**Scope:** Graph and graph-navigation interfaces in shipping products, standards and well-known tools, read for reusable design moves for the personal skill graph's Simple and Detailed views. Goes beyond [the earlier visual research](skill-graph-visual-research.md), which already covers Open Knowledge Maps, Kumu focus, Obsidian graph view, Cosmograph, InfraNodus, Graph Commons, WCAG and Bloom guardrails; none of that is repeated here. Research only; no application changes.
**Method:** Primary sources only: standards documents, product documentation, first-party posts by the people who built the thing, library documentation and source, papers by the technique's authors, and first-party screenshots. **Documented** means the source states it. **Observed** means it is visible in a first-party image that was downloaded and viewed during this research. **Proposal** means a design inference for Alexandria that needs validation. Sources that could not be fetched are listed under Limitations and are not used for claims unless flagged.

Constraints carried in from [the view-modes spec](skill-graph-view-modes-spec.md) and the earlier research: every unearned skill is Ready and never Locked; earned nodes show the earned Bloom number; node sizes stay uniform; the graph is still once settled; colour is never the only cue; no prerequisite route may be invented from layout. Colours and type come from the tokens in [`main.css`](../src/assets/css/main.css): eight mid-tone `--atlas-field-*` hues, neutral `--app-border` and `--app-muted-foreground`, Inter and JetBrains Mono.

## What to steal

Ranked by expected impact on the "templated" impression.

1. **Derive every dimension from one unit.** TfL builds a whole line diagram from the x-height of the station name: line thickness equals x, station ticks are 0.66x, parallel-line strokes 0.33x, corner radius 3x, interchange ring 0.5x ([TfL Line diagram standard](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-line-diagram-standard.pdf), documented and observed). *Apply to both views:* pick the skill-name x-height as the unit and express edge stroke, disc diameter, ring stroke, gaps and corner radius as multiples of it, so Simple and Detailed are visibly the same drawing at two scales.
2. **Let the connection carry the colour; keep the node neutral.** In TfL diagrams colour identifies a line and the colours are "mandatory and must be matched accurately" ([TfL Colour standard](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-colour-standard.pdf), documented); in commit graphs colour belongs to the lane, not the commit ([GitKraken interface guide](https://help.gitkraken.com/gitkraken-desktop/interface/), observed). *Apply to both views:* field colour goes on the rail or line that runs through a field's skills; discs, cards and regions lose their tints.
3. **Encode state in the form of the mark, not in chips or tints.** Vignelli's diagram legend: a solid dot means trains always stop, a hollow dot means they stop only outside rush hours, no dot means never ([The Vignelli Canon](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf), documented). GitKraken's legend uses ring, filled dot and dotted ring for commit, merge and work in progress (observed). *Apply to Simple and Detailed:* earned is a solid disc with the Bloom numeral, ready next is a heavy ring, ready is a thin ring. One mark per skill, identical in both views, with no extra badge.
4. **Hold edges to 45 and 90 degrees with one corner radius.** "Where a change of direction is necessary, the line must be drawn at an angle of 45 or 90 degrees", with a 3x inner radius (TfL Line diagram standard, documented). Cytoscape's taxi edges are right-angled with "at most two visible turns" and are described as appropriate for hierarchically laid-out DAGs ([Cytoscape.js](https://js.cytoscape.org/#style/taxi-edges), documented). *Apply to both views:* replace free Bézier curves with orthogonal or 45-degree routes and one shared radius.
5. **Put the graph in a narrow rail and typeset everything else as aligned text.** Fork and GitKraken draw the DAG in a gutter and set message, author, hash and date as plain aligned columns ([Fork](https://git-fork.com/), [GitKraken](https://help.gitkraken.com/gitkraken-desktop/interface/), observed). *Apply to Detailed:* a row per skill with the rail at the left and name, earned level and evidence as columns replaces the card atlas.
6. **Keep each lane straight.** GitKraken documents that "columns represent branches"; the author of the gitamine client compared six clients and chose straight branches because they are "much more readable" ([Commit graph drawing algorithms](https://pvigier.github.io/2019/05/06/commit-graph-drawing-algorithms.html), documented). *Apply to both views:* one field (or subject) owns one straight lane; only prerequisite edges that cross lanes bend.
7. **Emphasise by subtraction.** GitKraken: "Unrelated commits fade when a branch is hovered." GitLab: hovering a job highlights "every job that must run before the selected job" ([GitLab pipelines](https://docs.gitlab.com/ci/pipelines/#group-jobs-by-stage-or-needs-configuration), documented; observed: ancestor links turn blue and thicken, other jobs drop to roughly 40% opacity). *Apply to both views:* edges are thin neutral grey at rest; selection colours and thickens the full prerequisite chain and fades the rest. No glow, shadow or halo.
8. **Draw cross-field prerequisites as interchanges.** TfL interchange circles are "always printed in black with the centre left free", centred between lines that share a platform, and bridged with a white strip where a change is needed; flag boxes naming the connecting lines sit "always centred beneath the interchange circle and never above" (TfL Line diagram standard, documented and observed). *Apply to Simple:* a skill with prerequisites in another field gets a neutral interchange ring where the two field lines meet, and the connecting field is named in one fixed position.
9. **Make edges navigable.** Obsidian Canvas has "Follow connection": "The canvas moves to the card at the opposite end" ([Canvas help source](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/blob/master/en/Plugins/Canvas.md), documented). Linear: "Click on any dependency line to jump to a project" ([Linear project dependencies](https://linear.app/docs/project-dependencies), documented). Nx traces the chain between a chosen Start and End ([Nx explore graph](https://nx.dev/docs/features/explore-graph), documented). *Apply to navigation:* clicking a prerequisite edge moves selection to its other end; the inspector offers "trace from here to there".
10. **Collapse a group into one counted row with an explicit expand.** GitHub Actions folds a job matrix into "3/3 jobs are completed / Show all jobs" ([visualization graph](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/monitor-workflows/use-the-visualization-graph), observed). KeyLines combos have open and closed states and merge their links into one combo link ([KeyLines combos](https://cambridge-intelligence.com/combos/), documented). *Apply to Detailed and the overview:* a subject closes into "5 of 8 earned" with a single merged edge and opens in place.
11. **Offer two groupings of the same DAG behind a labelled switch, with connections as a separate toggle.** GitLab: "Group jobs by" Stage or Job dependencies, plus "Show dependencies" to render lines (documented and observed). Airflow pairs a Grid view ("each row represents a task") with a Graph view ([Airflow UI](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/ui.html), documented). *Apply to switching:* keep Simple and Detailed, and add a "Show connections" toggle so the default state can be nearly edge-free.
12. **Show only the neighbourhood asked for.** Bloom's scene "contains just the parts of the graph which you've found through search or exploration" ([Neo4j Bloom overview](https://neo4j.com/docs/bloom-user-guide/current/bloom-visual-tour/bloom-overview/), documented). Dagster selects "one layer upstream" with `1+key` ([asset selection syntax](https://docs.dagster.io/guides/build/assets/asset-selection-syntax/reference), documented). *Apply to navigation:* search lands on a skill and shows it with N layers of prerequisites and dependents, one control to widen.
13. **Snap positions to a small set of slots and angles.** Path of Exile places 1,325 nodes on fixed orbits holding 1, 6, 16, 16, 40, 72 and 72 slots at set angles such as 30, 45, 60 and 90 degrees ([official tree export](https://github.com/grindinggear/skilltree-export), [tree page](https://www.pathofexile.com/passive-skill-tree), documented). *Apply to both views:* a fixed grid pitch for discs and rows, so no position looks approximate.
14. **Mark what continues out of view.** TfL ends a line that leaves the diagram with an arrowhead (documented). `git log --graph-lane-limit` replaces overflow lanes with a `~` truncation mark ([git-log](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-log), documented). *Apply to focused or filtered views:* a prerequisite outside the current view ends in a short stub with an arrowhead and the field name, never silently dropped.

## What reads as templated, and what the crafted references do instead

| Trait in the current mock | What the references do | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A tinted, rounded card for every node | Nodes are marks on a line: a tick, a ring, a dot. Where cards exist (GitHub Actions) they are white, uniform, single-bordered, and the status icon is the only colour. | TfL standard (documented); GitHub Actions graph (observed) |
| Pill chips for metadata | Metadata is aligned plain text: columns in Fork and GitKraken, a right-aligned muted duration in GitHub Actions. TfL's flag boxes are the one boxed label, rectangular, in line colour only, in a fixed order and position. | Observed; TfL standard (documented) |
| Dashed borders as decoration | A dash means exactly one thing: not in service or not yet committed. TfL draws out-of-use track as dashes in 30% black; GitKraken reserves dotted outlines for work in progress and stashes. | TfL standard (documented); GitKraken legend (observed) |
| Coloured left border on a card | Colour runs along the connection itself and is the identity of the line or lane; it is never an accent stripe attached to a box. | TfL colour standard (documented); commit graphs (observed) |
| Soft pastel regions | Grouping uses position and one device at a time: a lane, a column, a hairline container, white space. Apple: use "negative space, container shapes, or separator lines" to show relation. | [Apple HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout) (documented); GitLab dependency view (observed) |
| One hue doing several jobs (field, state and accent) | "Avoid using the same color to mean different things." Reserve accents "for interactive elements, or important states"; "black or white are the best options for accent colors"; "make sure your graphs look good in greyscale". | [Apple HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [Cambridge Intelligence graph UX](https://cambridge-intelligence.com/graph-visualization-ux-how-to-avoid-wrecking-your-graph-visualization/) (documented) |
| Tints of unequal strength | Palettes are built for "consistent visual weight: at each level, no single color appears to take priority over another", using a perceptual colour space. | [Stripe accessible color systems](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems), [Linear redesign](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui) (documented) |
| Free Bézier curves at any angle | Two or three permitted angles and one radius; "short, straight edges are preferable to long, crooked ones". | TfL standard; [Gansner et al. 1993](https://graphviz.org/documentation/TSE93.pdf) (documented) |
| Emphasis by shadow, glow or halo | Emphasis by fading everything else and thickening the path. Linear explored hierarchy with "opacities of black and white". | GitKraken, GitLab, Linear redesign (documented) |
| Many type sizes, weights and small caps labels | One typeface and weight on the diagram (Johnston Medium, mixed case), label size tied to line thickness; "stick to one or two type sizes at the most"; "minimize the number of typefaces". | TfL standard, Vignelli Canon, [Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography) (documented) |
| Everything shown at the same volume | Routine items recede: Fork greys merge-commit rows; Bloom shows only what was found; "overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand". | Fork (observed); Bloom; [Shneiderman 1996](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Shneiderman1996eyes.pdf) (documented) |
| Spacing that looks approximate | Positions come from a unit or slot system: TfL's x, Path of Exile's orbits, a commit grid ("more pleasant to the human eye if the nodes are aligned"). | Documented |
| Several badges per node | One mark whose form carries the state (solid, hollow, absent). | Vignelli Canon (documented) |

**Proposal:** the common cause is decoration standing in for structure. Each crafted reference picks one structural device (line, lane, layer, slot), spends colour on identity only, and expresses state through the mark. The mock spends tint, radius, border and chip on the same facts several times over.

## Reference families

### 1. Transit and schematic maps

- **TfL Line diagram standard, Issue 4** (PDF fetched and read in full as text; pages 8 and 12 rendered and viewed). Documented: Johnston 100 Medium is "the only typeface used on all line diagrams"; construction "is based around the 'x' height of a station or stop name" and that height "is equal to the thickness of the route line"; station ticks are 0.66x square; a second class of line is drawn as two 0.33x strokes with a 0.33x gap instead of a new colour treatment; out-of-use sections are dashed and "always to be displayed in black 30 per cent"; direction changes only at 45 or 90 degrees with a 3x inner radius; lines leaving the diagram end in an arrowhead; interchange circles are black with a clear centre; flag boxes are ordered alphabetically, sit beneath the circle and never above, and print in the line colour with reversed type. Observed: the dimensioned figures show the tick, ring and bridging strip as plain geometric constructions with no outline, shadow or gradient. [Source](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-line-diagram-standard.pdf).
- **TfL Colour standard, Issue 11** (PDF fetched, text read). Documented: line and mode colours are a closed, named set with exact references, "mandatory", and consistent use "helps guide passengers to their destinations". [Source](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-colour-standard.pdf).
- **Vignelli's New York subway diagram** as reproduced with its legend in *The Vignelli Canon* (PDF hosted by the Vignelli Center, fetched, text read). Documented in the legend: "It is not a geographical map: relative distances have been altered in favor of better legibility"; "Stations on each line are indicated by dots"; local lines show a dot at every station and express lines only at express stations; solid, hollow and absent dots encode stopping pattern. [Source](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf).
- **MTA Live Subway Map** by Work & Co (designer's case study fetched). Documented: it combines "the geometric clarity of Massimo Vignelli's diagram with the geographical and organic curves by Hertz"; the map "dynamically redraws itself"; "Rather than using paragraphs of copy, service-change information is represented visually." [Source](https://work.co/clients/mta/).
- **Proposal:** a skill graph is closer to a line diagram than to a map. Even spacing, restricted angles and a per-field line are legitimate because distance carries no meaning in the taxonomy either. The limit is that a transit line implies an ordered route, and the earlier research forbids inventing routes; a field line may only pass through skills in an order the prerequisite records support.

### 2. Git and commit graphs

- **`git log --graph`.** Documented: the graph is drawn "on the left hand side of the output", implies topological order, can cap lanes with `--graph-lane-limit` (overflow shown as `~`), and indents visual roots so unrelated commits that are vertically adjacent do not look connected. The source keeps one column per in-flight parent with a colour index per column. [git-log](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-log), [graph.c](https://github.com/git/git/blob/master/graph.c).
- **GitKraken Desktop** (interface guide fetched; three first-party images viewed). Documented: "Each row is a commit"; "Columns represent branches, and lines show merges and relationships"; hovering a branch highlights related commits and fades the rest; hovering a commit shows its nearest containing "ghost" branch. Observed: lane colour continues into a faint row band that ends at the message text, so the eye can run from mark to label without a leader line; merge commits are small solid dots while ordinary commits are larger rings; connectors are vertical runs with rounded elbows; dotted outlines appear only on work-in-progress and stash nodes. [Source](https://help.gitkraken.com/gitkraken-desktop/interface/).
- **Fork** (first-party screenshot viewed). Observed: the graph occupies a gutter of a few lane widths; lanes are hairline coloured verticals with small hollow nodes; branch names are small outlined tags inline before the message; the selected row is one solid full-width fill; merge rows are greyed text; the detail pane below is label-and-value text with no boxes. [Source](https://git-fork.com/).
- **gitamine thesis chapter** by its author. Documented: clients differ on two choices, one commit per row and straight versus curved branches; placing nodes on a grid loses little "because it is more pleasant to the human eye if the nodes are aligned"; a child either continues a lane or merges into another, and that distinction decides which lane stays straight. [Source](https://pvigier.github.io/2019/05/06/commit-graph-drawing-algorithms.html).
- **GitHub network graph.** Documented: a timeline where "the first row references the date and the first column references the branch owner", limited to the 100 most recently pushed branches, with keyboard navigation and drag to reach older history. [Source](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/viewing-activity-and-data-for-your-repository/understanding-connections-between-repositories).
- **Proposal:** a row order for skills must be a topological order of prerequisites, grouped by field. The "branch child or merge child" rule maps to "first prerequisite in the same subject continues the lane; any other prerequisite is drawn as a merge elbow".

### 3. Node and canvas tools

- **React Flow (xyflow).** Documented: four edge types, default Bézier, straight, step and smoothstep; `<MiniMap />` "renders each node as an SVG element and visualizes where the current viewport is", defaults to grey nodes and a translucent mask, and is non-interactive unless `pannable` or `zoomable` is set; `<Controls />` is "a small panel" with zoom in, zoom out, fit view and lock; a first-party example shows a node choosing its content from the current zoom level. [Edge types](https://reactflow.dev/examples/edges/edge-types), [MiniMap](https://reactflow.dev/api-reference/components/minimap), [Controls](https://reactflow.dev/api-reference/components/controls), [Contextual zoom](https://reactflow.dev/examples/interaction/contextual-zoom).
- **FigJam connectors.** Documented: three kinds, bent ("wrap around objects"), curved and straight; lines snap to shape sides so "a shape brings its lines along with it". [Source](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500004414542-Create-diagrams-and-flows-with-connectors-in-FigJam).
- **tldraw.** Documented: arrows bind to shapes and have two routing modes, "arc for smooth curves controlled by a bend parameter, and elbow for right-angle routing that navigates around obstacles"; elbow leg length is a fixed value per size, not free. [Source](https://tldraw.dev/sdk-features/default-shapes).
- **Obsidian Canvas and JSON Canvas.** Documented: connections are directed by default with an arrow at the far end; "Follow connection" jumps to the other end; groups are named frames. The open format allows only four attachment sides and six preset colours whose values are "intentionally not defined so that applications can tailor the presets". [Canvas help source](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/blob/master/en/Plugins/Canvas.md), [JSON Canvas 1.0](https://jsoncanvas.org/spec/1.0/).
- **Proposal:** the default Bézier edge of canvas libraries is part of the generic look, because it is what every flow demo ships with. The tools themselves treat elbow routing as the structured option. A six-slot palette with app-defined values is the same idea as the `--atlas-field-*` tokens: closed set, meaning fixed, values owned by the theme.

### 4. Graph exploration tools

- **Cambridge Intelligence (KeyLines, ReGraph).** Documented: three failure shapes, hairballs, snowstorms and starbursts; the remedy for overcrowding is "progressive disclosure, giving 'detail on demand'"; "Apply consistent styling across the graph" and "Differentiate styling only when it adds important context"; "give your nodes room to breathe"; for labels, "smart truncation and tooltips", and avoid repeating words that appear on most nodes; put a colour key beside the graph on the same background colour. [Graph visualization UX](https://cambridge-intelligence.com/graph-visualization-ux-how-to-avoid-wrecking-your-graph-visualization/). Combos merge nodes and their links and have open and closed states. [Combos](https://cambridge-intelligence.com/combos/).
- **Neo4j Bloom.** Documented: the scene holds only what search or exploration has brought in; a legend panel lists categories; a search bar suggests graph patterns; a card list gives details for what is in the scene. [Source](https://neo4j.com/docs/bloom-user-guide/current/bloom-visual-tour/bloom-overview/).
- **Kiali.** Documented: "A single-click selects a graph node. A double-click drills in to show the node's Detail Graph", with a collapsible side panel summarising the selection and Find and Hide expressions. Its traffic animation is a deliberate non-reference for a graph that must stay still. [Source](https://kiali.io/docs/features/topology/).
- **Nx project graph.** Documented: "Viewing the entire graph can be unmanageable even for smaller repositories"; focus one project, then widen with proximity and group-by-folder controls; trace the chain between a Start and an End; click an edge to see what created it. [Source](https://nx.dev/docs/features/explore-graph).
- **Cytoscape.js.** Documented: compound parents take their size from their children; taxi edges are for "hierarchical, bundled edges" with a primary direction. [Source](https://js.cytoscape.org/).
- Kumu, Obsidian graph view and Cosmograph are covered in [the earlier research](skill-graph-visual-research.md).
- **Proposal:** "click an edge to see why it exists" suits prerequisites well: the edge inspector can show the prerequisite record and whether it is earned.

### 5. Learning and game progression graphs

- **Duolingo path.** Documented: the home screen changed from a tree of skills to "a path that you'll follow step by step"; each circle is one level; levels from different skills are interleaved; lessons are grouped into smaller units, each with a guidebook. The stated reason is guidance: learners should "be confident that each step you take … is truly the best step". [Source](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/). **Proposal:** this is evidence for a recommended-next reading of the graph, not for hiding the graph; Alexandria's Ready-never-Locked rule excludes a single forced path.
- **Path of Exile passive tree.** Documented: "a vast web of 1325 skills"; "All character classes share the same tree, but start at different locations". The official data export places nodes in groups on numbered orbits with fixed slot counts and angles, and group backgrounds are an explicit optional property. [Tree page](https://www.pathofexile.com/passive-skill-tree), [export](https://github.com/grindinggear/skilltree-export). **Proposal:** the lesson is geometric constraint at scale, and a shared map with different entry points per learner; the ornate art style does not transfer.
- Khan Academy's knowledge map, Metacademy, Brilliant, roadmap.sh and Civilization tech trees could not be verified from first-party sources in this pass (see Limitations).

### 6. Dependency and pipeline views

- **GitHub Actions workflow graph** (docs fetched, screenshot viewed). Documented: each job shows a status icon left of its name and lines indicate dependencies. Observed: uniform white cards on a pale grey canvas; one neutral grey edge colour with rounded orthogonal bends; small grey port dots where edges meet cards; durations right-aligned in muted text; parallel jobs stacked inside one card; a matrix collapsed into a tabbed group with a count; three small square controls (fit, minus, plus) at bottom right. The only saturated colour is the status icon. [Source](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/monitor-workflows/use-the-visualization-graph).
- **GitLab pipeline graph** (docs fetched, dependency-view hover screenshot viewed). Documented: group by Stage or by Job dependencies; "Show dependencies" renders lines; in the dependency view "jobs in the leftmost column run first"; hover highlights everything that must run before. Observed: each column is one bordered container holding plain rows of status icon, job name and a smaller muted stage name; idle links are pale grey; on hover the ancestor chain turns blue and unrelated rows fade. A mini graph gives one status mark per stage for use in lists. [Source](https://docs.gitlab.com/ci/pipelines/).
- **Airflow.** Documented: the Grid view is "the primary interface" with a row per task and a column per run; the Graph view shows "the logical structure". [Source](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/ui.html).
- **Dagster.** Documented: "A layer is a level of connection in the graph"; `+key` selects all upstream, `1+key` one layer. [Source](https://docs.dagster.io/guides/build/assets/asset-selection-syntax/reference).
- **Linear project dependencies.** Documented: dependencies are lines between bars on the timeline; "The blue line depicts a dependency that has not been violated. The red line is a dependency that has been violated"; clicking a line jumps to the project; the side panel lists "Blocked by" and "Blocking". [Source](https://linear.app/docs/project-dependencies).
- **Proposal:** edge colour as edge state (Linear) is a cleaner place for "prerequisite earned or not" than a node tint: an edge from an earned prerequisite can be solid field colour, an edge from an unearned one neutral. The GitLab mini graph is a model for the sidebar summary: one mark per field.

### 7. Layout and navigation research

- **Layered drawing.** Gansner, Koutsofios, North and Vo state four aesthetics for `dot`: expose hierarchy by aiming edges in one general direction; "avoid visual anomalies that do not convey information", for example crossings and sharp bends; keep edges short; favour symmetry and balance, noting they cannot all be optimised at once. [Paper](https://graphviz.org/documentation/TSE93.pdf) (fetched). ELK attributes the method to Sugiyama, Tagawa and Toda (1981), defaults to orthogonal routing, exposes "Favor Straight Edges Over Balancing", and supports compound graphs with cross-hierarchy edges. [ELK Layered](https://eclipse.dev/elk/reference/algorithms/org-eclipse-elk-layered.html). dagre offers `network-simplex`, `tight-tree` and `longest-path` rankers. [dagre wiki](https://github.com/dagrejs/dagre/wiki).
- **Overview, zoom and filter, details on demand.** Shneiderman's task list also includes relate, history and extract. [Paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Shneiderman1996eyes.pdf) (fetched).
- **Focus plus context.** Sarkar and Brown extend fisheye views to graphs: position, size and level of detail of each vertex depend on its distance from a clicked focus and on a preassigned importance. [Report](https://bitsavers.org/pdf/dec/tech_reports/SRC-RR-84A.pdf) (fetched). Furnas's degree-of-interest formulation is the origin ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/22627.22342), not fetched).
- **Edge bundling.** Holten's hierarchical edge bundles route adjacency edges along the containment hierarchy ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2006.147), not fetched). Cytoscape's taxi edges are a simple shipping form of the same idea.
- Animated transitions (Heer and Robertson) are covered in the earlier visual research; motion values for the app are in [the motion research](motion-design-research.md).
- **Proposal:** the Simple view's longest-path layering already satisfies the first aesthetic. The gap is the second and third: within-layer ordering by field then name ignores crossings, and wrapped rows create long edges. A crossing-reduction sweep within each field group, and straight-edge priority for same-lane prerequisites, would change the look more than any restyling. A distorting fisheye conflicts with the stillness rule; the usable part of the idea is level of detail by graph distance from the selection (full label and evidence at distance 0 and 1, name only at 2, mark only beyond).

### 8. What separates crafted from generic

- **Discipline as a rule set.** Vignelli: "Discipline is a set of self imposed rules, parameters within which we operate"; visual strength "should never be confused with just visual impact"; "It is really the white that makes the black sing"; "stick to one or two type sizes at the most"; primary shapes and colours are preferred "because their formal values are timeless". [The Vignelli Canon](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf) (fetched).
- **A standard instead of taste.** TfL publishes ratios, angles and exact colours, so two diagrams by different people look like one system (documented above).
- **Noise, alignment, hierarchy, density.** Linear's redesign aimed to "reduce visual noise, maintain visual alignment, and increase the hierarchy and density of navigation elements"; alignment work is described as "something that you'll feel after a few minutes"; the theme moved to LCH and was pulled back to "a more neutral and timeless appearance" by limiting how much of the brand hue enters surface colours, with text and neutral icons made darker in light mode. [Source](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui) (fetched).
- **Colour with equal weight.** Stripe set three goals for its palette: predictable accessibility, clear vibrant hues, and consistent visual weight, reached with a perceptual colour model. [Source](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems) (fetched).
- **Platform guidance.** Apple: "Avoid using the same color to mean different things"; "Align elements to make them easier to scan"; "Minimize the number of typefaces"; avoid light font weights; convey hierarchy with weight, size and colour. [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography) (page data fetched).
- **Proposal:** a graph UI looks designed when a reader could reconstruct its rules from the picture: one unit, a closed colour set with one meaning each, two or three permitted angles, one type size for labels, one mark vocabulary. It looks templated when each element is styled independently with whatever a component library offers. A short written standard for the skill graph, a page of ratios in the manner of the TfL document, would be the most durable output of the redesign.

## Design directions for Alexandria

All three are proposals. They share moves 1 to 4 and 7: one unit, colour on the connection, state in the mark, restricted angles, emphasis by subtraction.

### A. Line diagram

Each field is a coloured line running in the reading direction through its skills, drawn in the `--atlas-field-*` hue at one unit thick. **Simple:** skills are stations at even spacing. Earned is a solid neutral disc with the Bloom numeral, ready next a heavy ring, ready a tick or thin ring. Names are set horizontally in one size on a consistent side. A skill with a prerequisite in another field is an interchange ring joining the two lines. There are no regions, chips or tints; the field name is set once at the start of its line. **Detailed:** the same diagram at a larger scale, where each station gains a second line of plain text (earned level name and evidence) beneath its name, and the inspector opens as today. Switching views is a change of scale and label depth, which makes the existing travel transition almost trivial. **Main risk:** a transit line asserts an order. Fields whose prerequisites branch need forks and merges, and a field with few internal prerequisites would become an arbitrary sequence, which the earlier research forbids. This direction needs a rule that a line may connect two skills only where a prerequisite record exists, with unconnected skills drawn as separate short segments, and it needs a label-collision test on the full bundled taxonomy before commitment.

### B. Commit rail

**Detailed** becomes a ledger: one row per skill in topological order, grouped under plain field headings. A narrow rail at the left holds one straight lane per subject in the field colour, with the state mark on the lane and rounded elbows for prerequisites that arrive from another lane. To the right are aligned text columns: skill name, earned Bloom number and name, evidence, with Ready set as muted text. Selecting a row fills it, colours its ancestor and dependent chain in the rail, and fades other rows. **Simple** is the rail alone, turned to the reading direction and given room: lanes, marks and names with no columns. Cross-field prerequisites leave the field group as a stub with an arrowhead and the other field's name, and clicking the stub follows the connection. This direction is phone-native, since a list with a rail already fits a narrow screen, and it reuses the existing skill list for accessibility. **Main risk:** it stops looking like a map. Long cross-field edges are stubs rather than drawn lines, so the "whole graph at a glance" quality depends on Simple carrying it; and a hundred rows is long, so collapsed subject rows with counts (move 10) are required, not optional.

### C. Pipeline board

**Simple** keeps today's layered structure but restyles it as an instrument: each layer is a column, each field's skills in that layer sit in one hairline container, marks and names are plain rows, and edges are thin neutral orthogonal lines that are off by default behind a "Show connections" toggle. Hover or selection lights the chain. **Detailed** is the same board with wider rows that carry the evidence text, subjects collapsed into counted rows that open in place, and a quiet grey minimap for position. Field colour appears only as the mark colour for earned skills and as the lit-path colour. **Main risk:** this is the smallest departure from what exists, so it will only stop reading as templated if the card styling is removed completely and the unit and angle rules are enforced; a half-applied version lands back where the mock is. Columns by depth also separate skills of one field, which weakens field identity compared with A and B.

**Suggested combination (proposal):** B for Detailed and A's vocabulary for Simple, with one shared mark set and unit. It gives the largest visible change, fits phones, and keeps each view's purpose from the view-modes spec: a glance and an investigation.
